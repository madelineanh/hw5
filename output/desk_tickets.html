Reflection after the runs

1. How well did the agents perform on each ticket?

Ticket 101 · Bulldog tee Strong

The team got every fact right and made the one connection that mattered. Inventory found CC-TEE-WHITE size S at 0 and didn't stop there. It read invoice #501's description ("Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S") and worked out that the tee was already ordered and only stuck because Bulldog Print Co won't ship while the $840 invoice is 3 days overdue. Accounting checked the $3,400 balance, confirmed nothing had been paid, and prepared approval #1 instead of paying. When I clicked Approve, cash went to $2,560 and the invoice was marked paid.

Customer Service's draft to Tauhid was honest: sizes M (5), L (3), and XL (2) are available now, or wait for S "by September 5" if the supplier ships on August 31, "conditional and not guaranteed". It didn't swap sizes for the customer or mention the unpaid invoice.

The weak spot is cost. A one-tee order took 15 model calls, 22 tool calls, and 57,217 tokens.

Human approval: I approved approval #1 (invoice #501, $840 to Bulldog Print Co) on the dashboard as Madeline Ganey, and checking went from $3,400.00 to $2,560.00 (payment #1).

Ticket 102 · Rent due Strongest

This was the cleanest run. Facilities checked the landlord's email against lease 1: right landlord (Elm City Properties), $2,400 due 2026-09-02, exactly 2 days after the shop date, not overdue, and no rent already in payments. Then Accounting did the most impressive thing I saw. It noticed ticket 101's $840 was already pending, so it reported that the rent fits but leaves only $160 if both are approved. That is exactly where the Cash tab ended up. It took 11 model calls and 37,953 tokens, and no extra agents were involved.

Human approval: I approved approval #2 (lease 1 rent, $2,400 to Elm City Properties), and checking went from $2,560.00 to $160.00 (payment #2). The lease's next due date moved to 2026-10-02.

This ticket also shows why the guardrails mattered. Claude's first test of 102 from the dashboard (on a scratch copy, before my real run) failed. The Boss sent the rent ticket to Customer Service. Customer Service then asked Accounting to re-confirm the approval, Accounting escalated to a nested Boss, and the nested Boss hit the 6,000-token output cap and crashed the run. After the agent prompts were changed to trust facts a teammate had already verified, and the cap was raised, my real run was clean.

Ticket 103 · Bulk hoodie discount Good, but wordy

The numbers were all right. There are 8 size M hoodies on hand, so the order is 12 short. Accounting priced it with quote_bulk_discount: 15% off is $49.30 each, $986 for 20, a 55.4% margin. The deepest discount that keeps the 40% floor is 36.8% ($733.20), which needs a human. The Boss picked the 15% option, which is within policy. Accounting also saw invoice #501 was already pending as approval #1 and didn't queue it twice. That's a guardrail working across tickets.

It was the most expensive run, though: 20 model calls and 79,577 tokens. Accounting ran three quotes when two would do, and the Boss sent Customer Service two separate drafting requests. The plan also stays vague for the customer. The draft says "we do not have a firm restock date", and nobody proposed a concrete plan for the missing 12, such as how many hoodies the remaining cash could restock. In my earlier 1:52 pm terminal run, the Boss also flagged something I had missed: invoice #501 is for tees, not hoodies, so a person should confirm that paying it really unblocks the hoodie restock.

Human approval: none. The ticket's only money item, invoice #501, was already queued (and later approved) through ticket 101. A 12-hoodie restock would cost $264, which doesn't fit in the $160 left, so nothing was prepared and checking stayed at $160.00.

The difference is that the Boss called Accounting itself after reading Inventory's report, rather than Inventory passing the work on. The agents made the same decisions; only the route was different. The tools matched my table, plus prepare_payment and list_pending_approvals, which didn't exist when I wrote the plan (they came with Problem 7's "agents prepare, humans approve" change). I predicted the status waiting_on_approval; the real status was resolved, with the $840 waiting in the approval queue.

Ticket 102

I predicted Facilities first, then Facilities → Accounting, with no Inventory or Customer Service. Again the first contact and the agents left out matched, and again the Boss made the Accounting call itself instead of the specialist. Facilities used exactly the tools I listed (get_ticket, get_lease_rent_due, list_payments). Accounting went further than I expected: I predicted "$3,400 − $2,400 = $1,000 left", but it accounted for 101's pending invoice and correctly said $160.

Ticket 103

This matched my plan most closely in order: Boss → Inventory, Boss → Accounting, Boss → Customer Service, with no Facilities. The predicted numbers ($986, 36.8%) were exactly right. The differences were extra work: three quotes instead of two, and two Customer Service drafts instead of one. Overall my plans got the who right on all three tickets. The pattern I didn't predict is that the Boss acts as the hub and calls each specialist itself, so specialists never handed off to each other in the final runs.

3. What could one agent with tools have done more simply?

Ticket 102, the whole thing. The work is four tool calls: get_lease_rent_due(1), list_payments, get_cash_balance, prepare_payment('rent', 1). One agent could do that in about 3 model calls instead of 11. Two agents passing a report to each other added tokens, not judgment, because the lease row is the answer.

The Boss's routing. The Boss's first move was the same for every ticket type, and my Expected plans could predict it from the type field alone (customer_order and price_override → Inventory, rent_notice → Facilities). A simple rule in code could route tickets without spending a model call on "who should I call?"

Pricing in ticket 103. quote_bulk_discount already does all the math and policy checks (margin floor, max discount, needs-human flag). Wrapping it in an Accounting agent produced three quotes and a long report. One agent calling the tool once at 15% would have given the same $986 answer.

Customer drafts. Customer Service mostly turned facts it was handed into a polite email, and it redid the 103 draft because it was called before the discount was decided. If the Boss (or one agent) wrote the reply at the end, there would be no second round.

Where multiple agents did earn their keep was money. Keeping prepare_payment on Accounting only, with execution only through the human Approve button, made it impossible for, say, Customer Service to move cash. Accounting's habit of checking list_pending_approvals is what produced the correct $160 figure and stopped the duplicate invoice on 103.

  1. A customer orders Crest Mugs. CC-MUG-CREST is at 0 in Aisle D, the same situation as the tee. Inventory would find vendor 2, Elm City Gifts ("mugs and small goods", 3 lead days), and get_vendor_open_invoices(2) would show can_ship: true because it has no open invoices. Accounting could prepare_purchase 20 mugs at the $3.50 unit cost = $70, which fits in today's $160. Customer Service would draft an ETA of about September 3, and I would approve it on the dashboard.
  2. A second student group wants a bulk deal on Yale Caps. CC-HAT-BLUE has 40 on hand in Aisle C, so a 30-cap order needs no restock. Accounting's quote_bulk_discount would show cost $6 vs. list $22: 15% off is $18.70 each, $561 for 30, and the deepest discount that still keeps a 40% margin is about 54.5%. The Boss could offer 15% without a human, and Customer Service drafts the quote. This is ticket 103 again, but without the stock and vendor problems.
  3. The October rent notice arrives. Lease 1's next_due is now 2026-10-02 because ticket 102 moved it. Facilities would confirm the $2,400 notice, but Accounting's prepare_payment would refuse because $2,400 is more than the $160 left. The team can't fix that, but it would correctly raise the shortfall to the Boss instead of overdrawing the account, and the ticket would document that the shop needs money before Oct 2.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve today, and what it would need

  1. Taking the Yale AI Club's money. If the club accepts the $986 quote, there is no tool to record a sale, take payment, or reduce hoodie stock. cash_accounts can only go down (every payment tool subtracts), which is why the shop ended at $160 with no way back up. Needed: a Sales agent plus create_order, record_customer_payment (cash in), and reserve_or_ship_stock (lower inventory.qty) tools, an orders table, and human approval for refunds.
  2. Receiving the tee and hoodie restocks. Invoice #501 is paid now, but CC-TEE-WHITE S still says 0 and nothing tracks that the reprint should arrive about September 5. No agent can mark a shipment as received or add stock, so Tauhid's ticket can't be followed through to delivery. Needed: a purchase_orders table and place_purchase_order / receive_shipment tools that raise inventory.qty, and an Inventory agent with write access. Then 103's missing 12 hoodies could be ordered and tracked too, once cash allows.
  3. Actually talking to customers and the landlord. Tauhid has to choose M/L/XL or wait, and the AI Club has to pick 8 now or all 20 later. Both answers come by email, which the agents can't send or read; Customer Service's drafts just sit in the run output. Needed: a Communications agent with send_email (behind a human "Send" button, like Approve) and read_inbox / create_ticket_from_email, so a reply like "I'll take size M" becomes a new ticket the Boss can route to Inventory.
